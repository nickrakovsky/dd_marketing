## Reader promise and ownership

**Promise:** Identify what must be ready before a new physical warehouse starts operating, who owns each prerequisite, what evidence demonstrates readiness and which launch decisions remain unresolved.

| Reader | Outcome the article must support |
|---|---|
| Warehouse or project manager preparing a new site | Turn a list of purchases and activities into owned prerequisites, dependencies and evidence still needed. |
| Logistics or supply-chain director overseeing the launch | Understand unresolved constraints, resource decisions and accountable owners without needing to diagnose every operational detail. |
| Operations, inventory, facilities or IT lead contributing to the launch | See how their work connects to the receiving-to-dispatch operation and who accepts its readiness. |
| Small-site owner holding several responsibilities | Use the same structure without being required to invent separate departments or a formal project office. |

Primary intent is **warehouse setup checklist / new warehouse setup checklist**. Physical-warehouse launch readiness and opening requirements are closely related editorial language; their search demand is unverified. Use them where they describe the task, without promising to list every legal or technical requirement.

The audit pillar retains broad warehouse audit, audit checklist, audit forms/templates, operational assessment and inventory-audit ownership. The unqualified “warehouse checklist” is ambiguous and is not assigned wholesale to the new page. Daily work, routine inspections, stock registers, supplier selection, peak preparation and analytical data warehouses retain their separate meanings.

## Article structure

1. **Scope and checklist:** a short definition, the six readiness areas and direct navigation.
2. **Define the operation before specifying the setup:** expected goods, handling/storage needs, workload, service commitments, site constraints and the people who can confirm requirements. Record assumptions and unknowns.
3. **Work through the six readiness areas:** use the 18 starter prompts below, with an owner and evidence to obtain for each. Explain consequential dependencies rather than expanding into six separate technical manuals.
4. **Rehearse the intended workflow:** demonstrate a representative path from expected arrival through receipt, storage, order preparation and dispatch, including relevant exceptions. Use the site's stated acceptance criteria and competent owners; do not prescribe a universal transaction count or rehearsal duration.
5. **Record the launch decision and handover:** use the public record below to show evidence, gaps, resources and decision ownership. Include one short hypothetical example.
6. **Move into normal operation:** explain the handover to routine work and later assessment, with precise onward links. Initial readiness evidence does not establish continuing operating effectiveness.

Answer questions within the relevant section. Add an FAQ only if a useful question remains unanswered; avoid duplicating the article in a second format for search features.

## Starter checklist specification

Commission **18 starter prompts across six areas**. Each published prompt needs the prerequisite, a responsible role and a concrete example of evidence. The entries below specify coverage; production must verify any actual technical prescription. A role can hold several responsibilities, but the person who completes work and the person who accepts it should be identifiable.

| Area | Three prompts to develop | Ownership and evidence emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Operation and responsibilities — SET01–03** | Define the intended goods, workload and service commitments; identify launch scope, resources and dependencies; assign acceptance, decision and handover responsibilities. | Sponsor and operations lead: documented assumptions, agreed requirements, named owners and outstanding decisions. Avoid treating a target date as proof that the operation is ready. |
| **Site and applicable requirements — SET04–06** | Confirm suitability for the intended activities; establish required utilities, access and supporting facilities; identify applicable legal, product, customer and other requirements and prerequisite approvals. | Facilities and relevant competent/domain owners: requirements register, supporting assessments, service availability and approval evidence. The checklist does not supply universal local rules or grant approval. |
| **Layout and equipment — SET07–09** | Plan material flow, storage and staging for the intended work; provide equipment and storage appropriate to the requirements; establish installation/acceptance, maintenance and defect-response responsibilities. | Operations, facilities and equipment owners: reviewed plans, acceptance evidence, operating restrictions and maintenance arrangements. Ordered or delivered equipment is not automatically ready for use. |
| **Inventory and records — SET10–12** | Establish item, location and unit definitions; prepare and verify opening stock records where applicable; demonstrate records/tools, permissions and exception handling for the intended transactions. | Inventory and IT/process owners: reconciled setup data, appropriate access and transaction evidence. Paper, spreadsheets and WMS are possible approaches; purchasing a WMS is not a universal prerequisite. |
| **People and operating ownership — SET13–15** | Assign roles, coverage and escalation authority; establish relevant competence and practical preparation; make instructions, contacts and shift/customer/carrier handovers usable. | Operations and relevant training/safety owners: coverage plan, demonstrated capability, usable instructions and escalation contacts. Attendance alone does not demonstrate readiness to handle an exception. |
| **Receiving through dispatch — SET16–18** | Coordinate expected arrivals, receipt and disposition of exceptions; establish putaway, replenishment and order-preparation handoffs; establish release, dispatch documentation and carrier/customer handover. | Receiving, inventory and dispatch owners: demonstrated handoffs and traceable records across the intended workflow. Readiness at the receiving dock does not resolve an outstanding outbound dependency. |

Present six small groups or stacked check cards, not a wide spreadsheet squeezed into the article. Distinguish **activity completed**, **readiness demonstrated** and **decision outstanding** in plain language. Do not calculate a generic readiness percentage or use an overall green score that hides an unresolved prerequisite.

## Public launch decision record and example

Include a compact, selectable-text record readers can copy into their existing document or spreadsheet. Basic browser print should preserve it. This is an article element, not a new applet, generated report or downloadable workbook.

**Header:** site/launch scope; intended start date; goods and activities; expected operating conditions; applicable criteria and source of acceptance authority.

**Repeatable issue record:** prerequisite; evidence available and evidence missing; dependency; consequence for launch; next action; responsible owner; target date; resources or decision needed; decision owner.

**Decision and handover:** decision and supporting rationale; remaining constraints and prerequisite approvals; authorised scope; operating handover owner; follow-up/review arrangements. Recording a decision does not authorise waiving an applicable requirement or operating restriction.

Use a compact header, repeated issue blocks and a footer so the reader can use the record on a narrow screen. A custom copy button is unnecessary for the MVP. Keep decision authority distinct from action ownership.

Commission one **explicitly hypothetical** example: the site has configured its inventory tools, but a rehearsal produces a mismatch between the receipt record and the downstream stock record. The cause remains unconfirmed. The operations/inventory and IT owners identify the next evidence needed, record the affected launch dependency and specify who will review the result. The director sees the unresolved decision and resource need; the example does not invent a diagnosis, a successful retest or permission to launch.

This example teaches why a completed installation and demonstrated operating readiness differ. It must remain short and focused on pre-launch coordination; the audit pillar owns the extended investigation and corrective-action teaching examples.

## Boundaries and internal links

Shared subjects do not require duplicate answers. For equipment, setup asks what must be installed and accepted; audit asks what evidence shows the equipment controls work in the operating site. For inventory, setup establishes the initial records and workflow; audit examines discrepancies and control effectiveness. For governance, setup clarifies launch authority and dependencies; audit examines evidence, accountability and follow-through over the stated review period.

| Destination | Useful context and suggested link wording |
|---|---|
| [Warehouse audit checklist](https://datadocks.com/posts/warehouse-audit-checklist) | Opening distinction and/or post-launch handover: **assess an operating warehouse**. Do not reproduce its 28 checks, planner, examples or working pack. |
| [Receiving process](https://datadocks.com/posts/warehouse-receiving-process) | Receiving prerequisite: **define the receiving and exception-handling workflow**. The setup article establishes readiness; the linked article explains receipt steps. |
| [Warehouse safety checklist](https://datadocks.com/posts/warehouse-safety-checklist) | Relevant site/equipment/people prerequisite: **review warehouse safety arrangements**. Supporting orientation is not complete specialist guidance or launch approval. |
| [WMS selection](https://datadocks.com/posts/how-to-choose-a-warehouse-management-system) | Only where choosing a system is still a prerequisite: **choose a WMS against operating requirements**. Selection does not resolve configuration, testing or acceptance. |
| [Warehouse optimisation](https://datadocks.com/posts/warehouse-optimization-strategies) | Optional direction for an existing site's improvement task. Avoid repeating its layout, picking and optimisation checklist. |
| [Peak preparation](https://datadocks.com/posts/peak-season-without-dock-chaos-a-complete-playbook) | Optional direction for preparing an operating site for a seasonal surge. Do not broaden setup into the entire peak-planning task. |

Include links where the reader reaches that need, not as a compulsory six-link block. These destinations establish existing coverage; their technical and commercial claims are not independently validated by being linked. Receiving, safety, WMS selection and optimisation were reopened on 30 September. Peak's scoped role is carried forward from step 9.

Prioritise **audit → setup** in the audit page's explicit new-site direction. Consider **WMS selection → setup** only where the surrounding passage concerns opening a physical warehouse. Activate incoming links once the new page is published. No site-wide linking campaign is required. Keep both pages at distinct URLs; this proposal requires no redirect or canonical consolidation.

A relocation project may use the destination-readiness section, but this article is not a complete move plan. A WMS-only replacement needs its own implementation/cutover work; a new warehouse checklist does not replace it. Capacity assumptions inform the setup without becoming a network-design or capital-investment business case. Signpost these limits briefly instead of commissioning additional pages.

## Acceptance and evaluation

Before publication, verify the source of each substantive claim, competent review where needed, working links, rendered headings, mobile readability and basic print output. Confirm that the public checklist/record are in readable HTML and that the published article has the site's normal indexable article configuration. No new special AI markup is prescribed.

Use the following scenarios to evaluate the draft; these are proposed checks, not tests already conducted:

| Scenario | Required result |
|---|---|
| Equipment delivered but acceptance evidence missing | Reader identifies the outstanding prerequisite, owner and launch consequence; “delivered” is not treated as ready. |
| System installed but transactions fail in rehearsal | Reader records the dependency and operations/IT responsibility; a system-selection link does not falsely resolve it. |
| Receipt exception or incomplete outbound handoff | Reader can name the recording, disposition and decision responsibilities still to establish across the full workflow. |
| Training attended but an exception cannot be handled | Reader distinguishes attendance from demonstrated capability and identifies the next preparation/review step. |
| Director sees an incomplete launch plan | Reader can state consequential gaps, owners, resources and decision authority without a misleading overall readiness score. |
| Visitor needs an operating-site audit, peak plan or WMS-only cutover | The scope distinction is clear; the visitor is not led to believe this checklist completes a different task. |

Carry forward the page-set evaluation method. Preserve the audit query groups and historical export; obtain comparable page/query baselines for both URLs where available. Track explicit setup queries and relevant emerging variants on the new page, and their distribution across both pages. Protect the audit page's Q01/Q02/Q03/Q08 intent rather than celebrating a combined total that conceals erosion of its core queries.

Check access/indexing and gross routing errors after release, then review comparable periods around 4 and 12 weeks. Those are observation checkpoints, not deadlines for ranking success. Report raw counts, context and small-sample limits; one or two clicks do not establish uplift or cannibalisation. Assess whether users can identify a missing prerequisite and next decision. Treat relevant enquiries as an additional outcome, without imposing an unsupported lead forecast.
