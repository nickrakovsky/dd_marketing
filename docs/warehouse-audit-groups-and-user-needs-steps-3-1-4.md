## Step 3.1 — Group by the main purpose of the audit

The suggested groups work. Use **operational focus**, **compliance and assurance focus**, and **other focus**, with the latter explicitly identified as commercial decision-making or cost recovery where applicable. Adding “assurance” makes room for governance, financial reliability, voluntary standards, customer requirements and protective controls without implying that all are statutory obligations.

| Group | Main question | Typical intended result |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational (O)** | How well does the warehouse work, and what should improve? | More accurate, efficient, reliable execution; a prioritised improvement plan. |
| **Compliance and assurance (C)** | Are requirements and responsibilities being met, and can we substantiate that? | Evidence of conformity, reliable records, effective controls, risk treatment and corrective action. Includes safety, legal, contractual, governance and voluntary commitments. |
| **Other — commercial (X)** | Are we paying correctly, recovering money owed, or making a defensible provider decision? | A supported claim, corrected charges or a commercial decision. This is not a residual category for low-value users. |

These are analytical groupings, not an industry-standard taxonomy. Classify by the engagement’s dominant purpose, not the job title of the person searching or the department funding it. Most audits support more than one purpose. Safety and security remain under C in the broad sense requested, even when no formal certification is sought.

| L03 | Financial inventory/internal controls/fraud | **C** | Separate financial-reporting assurance from internal-control review or investigation; their methods and audiences differ. |

Do not infer market size or priority from the number of rows in each group. The taxonomy is more granular in specialist assurance contexts. Nor does a financial sponsor imply X: financial reporting and custody assurance remain C. L08 is X because its main intended result is payment verification or recovery.

## Step 4 — Who uses the phrase, what hurts, and what they need to learn

### Evidence and interpretation

The sources substantiate audit scopes, workflows, responsibilities and some concrete triggers. **The role combinations, likely pain points and educational needs below are reasoned hypotheses, not validated searcher personas.** No interviews, query analytics or observed search sessions were available or used. A source establishes that an audit happens; it does not prove which person enters “warehouse audit.”

Titles vary with company size and jurisdiction. In a small operation one person may hold several roles. “Sponsor” means whoever commissions or relies on the work; it does not imply that the searcher is a software buyer. Regulators may initiate examinations independently of the business. Examples from one jurisdiction are not universal requirements.

### L01 — Overall operations and performance — O

**People and situation:** A warehouse/DC manager or continuous-improvement lead conducts or prepares the assessment; an engineer or consultant may review it; an operations/supply-chain director commissions or relies on it.

**Likely pain points:** Capacity pressure, overtime, slow fulfilment and inconsistent productivity, with uncertainty about whether processes, layout, staffing or technology cause the problem.

**Information needs:** Define scope; collect baseline data and floor observations; distinguish symptoms from causes; make fair comparisons; quantify and prioritise improvements; assign ownership and verify results. A novice also needs to know what an audit should produce.

**Basis:** [Establish](https://www.establishinc.com/warehouse-operations-audit) documents the assessment scope. [Prological](https://www.prologicalconsulting.com/case_study/warehouse-audit/) gives a concrete case of capacity pressure and expensive additional labour.

### L02 — Inventory and stock accuracy — O

**People and situation:** Inventory controller, stock manager or warehouse supervisor organising verification; counters and a supervisor resolve differences. Operations relies on accurate availability; finance may separately request supporting evidence.

**Likely pain points:** Missing stock, wrong locations, picking shortages, repeated recounts, disruptive stocktakes and adjustments that hide recurring errors.

**Information needs:** Stocktake versus cycle count; scope and sampling; location, batch and unit handling; controlling movements during counts; blind counts/recounts; investigating variances before authorised adjustments; measuring accuracy.

**Basis:** [Microsoft’s cycle-counting documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/supply-chain/warehousing/cycle-counting) distinguishes work creation, counting and difference resolution. [Sentispec](https://inventory.sentispec.com/) supplies the established warehouse-audit/stock-verification usage.

### L03 — Financial inventory, internal controls and fraud — C

**People and situation:** Inventory accountant/controller and warehouse manager prepare records; an external financial auditor or internal auditor examines them. CFO, audit committee or public oversight body relies on the findings.

**Likely pain points:** Year-end evidence gaps, unsupported adjustments, unreliable balances, weak custody controls, unresolved findings or suspected diversion.

**Information needs:** Count versus audit; financial assurance versus control review; reconciliation, ownership and period-boundary evidence; third-party stock confirmation; segregation of duties; documenting remediation. Suspected fraud adds questions about investigation responsibility and preserving evidence.

**Basis:** [PCAOB AS 2510](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2510) concerns inventory observation and custodian evidence, not every accounting assertion. [Martin County’s audit](https://www.martinclerk.com/DocumentCenter/View/856/Utilities-Warehouse-Audit-Report---2021-PDF) records adjustment and control weaknesses.

### L04 — Workplace safety and fire — C

**People and situation:** Warehouse/site manager preparing; health-and-safety lead or competent assessor reviewing; employer/operations leadership accountable. An official inspection can be initiated by a regulator.

**Likely pain points:** Near misses, changing layouts/equipment, unresolved hazards, unclear responsibilities and approaching inspections, alongside pressure to keep work moving.

**Information needs:** Audit versus risk assessment versus specialist inspection; applicable jurisdiction and scope; identifying hazards; required competence; evidence and incident records; prioritising and closing corrective actions.

**Basis:** [OSHA’s warehouse standards and enforcement overview](https://www.osha.gov/warehousing/standards-enforcement) and [HSE’s warehouse safety guide](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg412.pdf). These are contextual sources, not a combined universal checklist.

### L05 — Racking and storage-equipment condition — C

**People and situation:** Facilities/warehouse manager or designated racking-safety owner; trained internal checker and specialist rack inspector have different responsibilities. Site management authorises remedial work.

**Likely pain points:** Impact damage, uncertain load capacity, missing inspection history and unclear repair urgency; risk of collapse or unnecessarily closing storage areas.

**Information needs:** Routine reporting versus expert inspection; choosing a competent inspector; recognising issues requiring escalation; understanding isolation/unloading decisions; repair and condition records.

**Basis:** [HSE storage guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg246.pdf), especially racking inspection. This supports the need for role and competence distinctions, not instructions to make structural judgments from a generic template.

### L06 — Housekeeping, 5S and 6S — O

**People and situation:** Area/team leader maintains standards; a Lean lead or rotating assessor reviews them; warehouse management sponsors the programme, with safety involvement for 6S.

**Likely pain points:** Clutter, missing tools, inconsistent labels, standards decaying between inspections, arbitrary scores and repeat findings without follow-up.

**Information needs:** Observable warehouse examples for each S; consistent scoring and evidence; responsibilities and cadence; sustained standard work rather than a one-off tidy-up; checking that actions stay effective.

**Basis:** [Optioryx’s 5S/6S warehouse audit account](https://www.optioryx.com/blog/operational-audits-in-warehousing-5s-and-6s-audits) supports the workflow and management roles; it is a provider’s account rather than independent demand research.

### L07 — Third-party warehouse qualification and performance — C/O; sometimes X

**People and situation:** Customer-side logistics/contract manager, procurement lead or supplier-quality specialist assesses the provider; the 3PL site manager prepares evidence. Supply-chain or purchasing leadership makes appointment/continuation decisions.

**Likely pain points:** Unverified capability claims before appointment, unsuitable product handling, repeated service failures, unclear contractual expectations and ineffective corrective actions.

**Information needs:** Separate qualification from performance improvement; determine product-specific criteria; compare questionnaires with observed practice; substantiate capacity and service claims; interpret measures and contractual commitments; verify correction.

**Basis:** [CBA guidance](https://www.chemical.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Warehouse_Audit_Guidance_Version1_-_June_2016.pdf) establishes pre-appointment and ongoing due diligence. [FreightOptics](https://www.freightoptics.com/blog/how-to-conduct-a-3pl-warehouse-audit-in-2026-a-checklist-for-every-critical-function/) supports the service-performance context.

### L08 — Warehousing billing and charges — X

**People and situation:** Logistics-cost analyst, accounts-payable/finance analyst or contract manager reconstructs charges; a specialist auditor may assist. Finance/operations leadership commissions the review; the provider’s billing team explains discrepancies.

**Likely pain points:** Rising invoices, opaque fees, confusing billing units, rate changes, disputed quantities and difficulty matching activity-based charges to evidence.

**Information needs:** Which invoices, rate-card versions and activity records to obtain; how billing units work; reconstruct quantities; distinguish legitimate changes from errors; support disputes and follow credits to resolution.

**Basis:** [Audity](https://www.audity.com.au/) documents warehouse-charge analysis and a claim deliverable. Its recovery claims are not assumed typical.

### L09 — Cargo security and loss prevention — C

**People and situation:** Site/security or loss-prevention manager prepares; customer supply-chain security staff or an approved auditor reviews; shipper or logistics-provider leadership relies on assurance.

**Likely pain points:** Theft, attempted intrusion, vulnerable goods, unclear control ownership and customer demands for credible evidence.

**Information needs:** Applicable standard and facility scope; evidence for physical and procedural controls; gap review versus formal certification; assessor requirements; recording and closing weaknesses.

**Basis:** [TAPA Facility Security Requirements](https://tapaemea.org/standards-trainings/facility-security-requirements/) establishes the facility security and certification context. The primary grouping also covers non-certification loss-prevention reviews.

### L10 — Food and product-safety assurance — C

**People and situation:** Quality/food-safety or technical manager prepares with warehouse management; internal, customer or certification-body auditor reviews; brand/retailer or site leadership relies on it.

**Likely pain points:** Contamination or storage-condition concerns, complaints, certification deadlines and gaps between written procedures and actual practice.

**Information needs:** Relevant product/activity scope; risk-based controls; records, observation and interview evidence; self-assessment; handling nonconformities and root causes; demonstrating sustained correction.

**Basis:** [BRCGS Storage and Distribution certification guide](https://www.brcgs.com/media/1axllyiz/guide-to-certification-storage-distribution-screenready.pdf). Product damage reduction alone can be operational; this row addresses assurance of product safety and quality.

### L11 — Pharmaceutical storage and GDP — C

**People and situation:** Quality/GDP manager or Responsible Person where applicable; warehouse manager supplies evidence; supplier auditor or regulatory inspector reviews. Licence holder or pharmaceutical client relies on the outcome.

**Likely pain points:** Inspection readiness, temperature excursions, incomplete records, unclear delegated duties, returns/recalls and product-integrity risk.

**Information needs:** Applicable licence/jurisdiction; GDP scope; responsibilities; storage/temperature evidence; traceability; technical agreements; self-inspection; deviations, corrective actions and recall controls.

**Basis:** [MHRA wholesale-distribution guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67ea784e0678ace40a7f275c/GN_6_Brexit_changes-GDP.pdf), including sections 7 and 9. The UK Responsible Person role is not treated as a universal title.

### L12 — Chemical and dangerous-goods storage — C

**People and situation:** Goods-owner logistics/EHS specialist assesses a provider; warehouse EHS/site manager prepares; specialist adviser assists where relevant.

**Likely pain points:** New product mixes, incompatible storage, spills/fire risks and unclear contractor responsibilities.

**Information needs:** Product-specific scope; safety-data interpretation; segregation, containment and emergency evidence; staff competence; provider suitability and escalation of serious gaps.

**Basis:** [CBA warehouse-audit guidance](https://www.chemical.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Warehouse_Audit_Guidance_Version1_-_June_2016.pdf). Its detailed criteria are specialised and historical; this analysis identifies learning needs rather than prescribing current legal requirements.

### L13 — Customs and bonded warehousing — C

**People and situation:** Warehousekeeper, trade-compliance manager or bonded-stock administrator prepares records with warehouse staff; customs authorities examine them, sometimes after an internal readiness review.

**Likely pain points:** Authorisation/supervisory checks, unclear goods status, stock discrepancies, missing movement evidence and changes to recordkeeping systems.

**Information needs:** Jurisdiction-specific duties and role boundaries; receipt-to-discharge audit trail; customs status; stock/declaration reconciliation; records needed to explain exceptions.

**Basis:** [HMRC warehouse authorisation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/apply-to-operate-a-customs-warehouse) and [duty-management guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/special-procedure-customs-warehousing/duty-management-and-closing-stock-balance-systems). The business may prepare for an examination it did not commission.

### L14 — Commodity custody, receipts and collateral — C

**People and situation:** Commodity warehouse operator, stock-records manager and finance administrator prepare; government examiner or independent custody auditor reviews. Depositors, owners, lenders and regulators rely on different parts of the findings.

**Likely pain points:** Uncertain stock coverage, quantity/condition discrepancies, receipt obligations, licensing examinations, financing requests and doubts about loss protection.

**Information needs:** Distinguish ordinary inventory records from custody/receipt obligations; reconcile quantities and commitments; document ownership and condition; understand relevant licensing/insurance expectations; establish acceptable lender/depositor evidence.

**Basis:** [WSDA’s warehouse-audit overview](https://agr.wa.gov/services/inspections-and-investigations/inspections/grain-inspection/grain-warehouse-audit-overview) and [PCAOB AS 2510](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2510). Regulated-warehouse compliance and collateral assurance are related but distinct assignments.

### L15 — WMS and operational systems/data — O

**People and situation:** WMS administrator/superuser and operations owner prepare; systems analyst, integration specialist or WMS consultant reviews; IT and supply-chain leadership sponsor decisions.

**Likely pain points:** Manual workarounds, poor process fit, inconsistent data, integration failures, weak reports and uncertainty over reconfiguration versus replacement.

**Information needs:** Map processes to system behaviour; trace transactions across interfaces; inspect configuration and data; separate training/process problems from software limits; specify and evaluate remedies. A controls-focused audit adds access, approval and traceability questions.

**Basis:** [KPI warehouse audits](https://kpisolutions.com/consulting/warehouse-audits/) and [WMS services](https://kpisolutions.com/consulting/warehouse-management-systems/). This remains distinct from an analytical data-warehouse audit.

### L16 — Individual order, pack or shipment verification — O

**People and situation:** Outbound quality checker, packing lead or assigned operative performs checks; shipping/quality supervisor reviews exceptions; WMS administrator configures selection rules.

**Likely pain points:** Mis-picks, complaints, repeat discrepancies and tension between checking enough shipments and delaying dispatch.

**Information needs:** What to compare; which shipments to select; handling extras, shortages and wrong items; recording corrections and authorising release; identifying recurring error patterns.

**Basis:** [Oracle Outbound Audit](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/warehouse-management/25d/owmol/outbound-audit.html) and [audit-rule documentation](https://docs.oracle.com/en/cloud/saas/warehouse-management/21b/owmol/onlinehelp/topics/outbound_audit_rules_screen.html). The task is established; these people’s use of the bare phrase remains a hypothesis.

### L17 — Environmental performance and energy use — O/C

**People and situation:** **Efficiency:** facilities/energy manager and engineer, with finance/operations sponsoring investment. **Assurance:** environmental/EHS or sustainability manager prepares; internal or certification auditor reviews; management/customer relies on evidence.

**Likely pain points:** Efficiency: high bills, poor measurement and uncertain savings. Assurance: unclear obligations, weak environmental records and unclosed findings.

**Information needs:** Efficiency: baseline, meters and operating conditions, saving opportunities and verification. Assurance: significant impacts, applicable obligations, control evidence, audit scope and corrective actions. Distinguish an energy assessment from management-system certification.

**Basis:** [ISO energy management](https://www.iso.org/iso-50001-energy-management.html) distinguishes improvement from optional certification. [ISO environmental management](https://www.iso.org/standard/14001) supports the performance/obligations distinction. Warehouse-specific occurrence was established in steps 2–3.

### L18 — Labour, social and ethical compliance — C

**People and situation:** Warehouse HR/compliance or site manager prepares; buyer’s responsible-sourcing lead commissions; independent social auditor assesses. Workers and representatives are evidence participants, not merely management’s subjects.

**Likely pain points:** Concerns about hours, pay, recruitment or treatment; customer audit requests; fragmented agency-labour records; repeat audits and difficulty closing findings.

**Information needs:** Applicable law/code and scope; worker interviews, records and observations; agency/subcontracted labour; corrective-action follow-through; report sharing; audit versus certification. SMETA specifically is an audit methodology, not certification.

**Basis:** [Sedex SMETA](https://www.sedex.com/solutions/smeta-audit/). Role/need inference is stronger than evidence that this audience routinely uses the bare phrase.

## Cross-cutting findings within step 4

1. **Task position changes the information need.** Someone conducting an audit needs scope, method, evidence and judgment criteria. Someone preparing for one needs responsibilities, records and readiness. Someone commissioning or relying on it needs scope, assessor competence and an interpretable result. A person closing findings needs root-cause and verification guidance.
2. **Job title alone does not identify intent.** The same warehouse manager can seek operational improvements one day and prepare evidence for a customer, safety, financial or regulatory audit the next.
3. **Experience changes the starting point.** An employee newly assigned the task may need terminology and sequencing; a specialist may need authoritative criteria and evidence requirements. Junior staff, trainees or consultants can be the researcher without owning the eventual decision. These are inferred possibilities, not measured audience shares.
4. **Recurring needs are specificity and follow-through.** Likely questions include what is in scope, which criteria apply, what counts as evidence, how to minimise disruption, and how to turn findings into verified correction. Their answers differ materially across the eighteen uses.
