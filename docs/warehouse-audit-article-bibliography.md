# Working bibliography for the warehouse audit article

Prepared **30 September 2026; updated 1 October for reader/content corrections** to [Post 1](warehouse-audit-article-draft.md). This selected authoring bibliography contains eleven independent/practitioner/official sources plus one separately labelled owned product reference. It is not a claim that each proposed checklist item has been externally validated. Turn 2 adds the blind-counting and scoped customs sources, three checked quotation selections and their provenance ledger.

The selection combines academic fieldwork, an actual warehouse audit report, an operator's account and institutional/official guidance. These represent different kinds of evidence. Corporate claims and prescribed procedures are not treated as observations of effectiveness. The 28-check framework, billing-record chain and hypothetical examples are our editorial synthesis, with further source needs identified below.

## B01 Warehouse assessment through observation and questions

**de Koster, M. B. M. (2008).** “Warehouse assessment in a single tour.” In M. Lahmar (ed.), *Facility Logistics: Approaches and Solutions to Next Generation Challenges*, pp. 39–60. Auerbach–Taylor & Francis. [University-hosted manuscript](https://www.eur.nl/en/erim/media/2025-08-warehouseassessmentacad).

- **Type and role:** Academic assessment method; useful context for commissioning and executing a review.
- **Passages:** Manuscript pp. 2–4, introduction/method and questions for workers; pp. 11–12, results, validation and conclusion.
- **Supports:** Combining observation and questions; considering several performance dimensions; recognising constraints outside a warehouse manager's authority.
- **Limits:** Approximate tour-based assessment with limited validation and assessor dependence; financial performance is excluded. It does not validate our checklist, an overall score or a compliance conclusion.

**Version note:** This manuscript identifies the 2008 chapter above. The [2012 repository record](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/84570/) concerns a different book chapter in *Warehousing in the Global Supply Chain*, pp. 457–473, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4471-2274-6_17. Do not attach that DOI or date to quotations from the inspected 2008 manuscript. This clarifies the version distinction in the earlier evidence register.

## B02 Records for tracing warehouse movements

**Logistics Cluster. (n.d.).** “Warehousing Documentation.” *Logistics Operational Guide*. [Full guidance](https://log.logcluster.org/en/warehousing-documentation). Accessed 30 September 2026; the footer's copyright year is not treated as the publication date.

- **Type and role:** Institutional operational guidance for humanitarian logistics; evidence prompts for inventory and receiving/dispatch.
- **Passages:** Opening scope paragraphs; “Waybill/Delivery Note”; “Goods Received Note”; “Goods Release Note/Stock Release Order”; “Stock / Stack / Bin Card”; “Inventory Ledger.”
- **Supports:** Following incoming/outgoing goods through transaction and stock records; adapting record content to the operation; distinguishing a physical stock record from a central ledger.
- **Limits:** Humanitarian operating context. Do not universalise its form names, physical-card requirements or update frequencies. It does not prove that a record is correct or that software prevents errors.

## B03 Reception and dispatch coordination

**Logistics Cluster. (n.d.).** “Goods Flow Management.” *Logistics Operational Guide*. [Full guidance](https://log.logcluster.org/en/goods-flow-management). Accessed 30 September 2026.

- **Type and role:** Institutional operating guidance; context for handoffs and the conditional coordination discussion.
- **Passages:** “Planning Reception,” “At Cargo Reception,” “Planning Dispatch” and “At Cargo Dispatch.”
- **Supports:** Communicating anticipated deliveries, recording receipt discrepancies, establishing release authority and updating movement records.
- **Limits:** Describes recommended humanitarian procedures. It does not establish that scheduling caused an error, that a particular product fixes it, or that its notice periods and rejection rules fit every warehouse. No quantitative benefit claim is supported.

## B04 Warehouse safety responsibilities and inspection evidence

**Health and Safety Executive. (2007).** *Warehousing and storage: A guide to health and safety*. Second edition, HSG76. ISBN 9780717662258. [Official publication record](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/hsg76.htm); [full PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg76.pdf).

- **Type and role:** Official British guidance developed with industry and worker representatives; context for equipment, workplace safety and follow-through.
- **Passages:** Paragraphs 18–22, management and priorities; paragraph 245, checking whether action was effective; paragraphs 641–652, racking inspection, responsibility and records. Racking discussion appears on PDF pages 115–116, counting the first PDF page as 1.
- **Supports:** Identifying responsible people, recording defects/inspections and distinguishing routine observation from competent assessment.
- **Limits:** A 2007 publication, still hosted by HSE, not verification of every current legal reference or a global rulebook. Any precise technical/legal instruction needs a current, applicable source and review. No universal rack-damage threshold is adopted.

**Scope clarification:** [HSG246](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg246.pdf), referenced in earlier research, specifically concerns steel and other metal stock. HSG76 is the broader warehouse source for this manuscript. Do not transfer metal-stock-specific instructions into a general checklist.

## B05 An actual warehouse audit and its follow-up findings

**Martin County Clerk of the Circuit Court & Comptroller. (2021, August 16).** *Utilities Warehouse Inventory*. Audit Report 2021-A-0001. [Official report PDF](https://www.martinclerk.com/DocumentCenter/View/856/Utilities-Warehouse-Audit-Report---2021-PDF).

- **Type and role:** Primary public-sector audit report; concrete evidence for financial controls, contractor oversight and verification of corrective work.
- **Passages:** pp. 7–8, recommendations 2–3 on verifying earlier action; p. 11, recommendation 12 on contractor oversight; pp. 12–13, sample and transaction comparisons; pp. 22–25, observations 1–2/recommendations 1–5 on adjustments and investigation; pp. 28–29, scope and methods. Printed page numbers match one-based PDF pages.
- **Supports:** Why supporting explanations, count/movement records and evidence of implementation matter. Distinguishes reported action from verified implementation.
- **Limits:** One municipal utilities operation and a judgmental sample. Do not generalise its error rates, tolerances or recommendations; management responses do not establish later effectiveness. Avoid using discrepancy totals without reconciling the report's differently scoped counts.

## B06 Financial audit work includes quantities and physical condition

**Public Company Accounting Oversight Board. (n.d.).** *AS 2510: Auditing Inventories*. [Official standard](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2510). Accessed 30 September 2026.

- **Type and role:** Primary auditing standard; corrects the existing article's over-simple split between operational counting and financial valuation.
- **Passages:** .02, scope limitation; .09, quantities/condition and count methods; .10–.12, records, observation and intervening transactions; .14, outside custodians.
- **Supports:** The scoped point that financial audit inventory work can include physical quantities and condition, with records and observation serving distinct purposes.
- **Limits:** Applies within the relevant auditing framework, not to every warehouse review. The standard itself is limited to inventory observation; it does not supply our complete method or all financial assertions. The displayed 2002 AICPA copyright is not a verified publication date for the current standard.

## B07 One operator describes several kinds of warehouse audit

**United Warehouse. (n.d.).** “Warehouse Facility Audits.” [Operator account](https://www.unitedwarehouse.com/why-united/quality-assurance/audits/). Accessed 30 September 2026. The browser title is “Audits – United Warehouse”; the substantive page heading is used here.

- **Type and role:** Warehouse operator's public self-description; a practical example for compliance, customer assurance and mixed reviews.
- **Passages:** Opening paragraph; “Self audits”; “Third-party audits”; “Customer audits”; “Other Facility Audits,” second paragraph.
- **Supports:** An attributed account of internal, third-party, customer/customer-of-customer and insurance-related reviews at this operator.
- **Limits:** Not independent inspection records, proof of current certification or observed outcomes. Its stated cadence is not an industry recommendation. The copyright footer does not establish a publication date.

## B08 Operational improvement and business performance

**Abushaikha, Ismail, Loay Salhieh and Neil Towers. (2018).** “Improving distribution and business performance through lean warehousing.” *International Journal of Retail & Distribution Management*, 46(8), 780–800. [DOI 10.1108/IJRDM-03-2018-0059](https://doi.org/10.1108/IJRDM-03-2018-0059); [university record](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5801/); [accepted manuscript](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5801/1/5801%20Towers%20%282018%29%20Improving%20distribution%20and%20business%20performance%20through%20lean%20warehousing.pdf).

- **Type and role:** Empirical academic study; a brief qualification when connecting operating changes to business outcomes.
- **Passages:** Manuscript §3.3, pp. 18–19, sample; §4, pp. 24–26, findings/mediation; §6, pp. 28–29, limitations. Page numbers are manuscript pages, not journal pagination.
- **Supports:** Positive associations with warehouse/distribution performance; a nonsignificant direct waste-reduction–business-performance relationship and modelled indirect relationships.
- **Limits:** 270 responses aggregated across 90 firms in five Middle Eastern countries; purposive survey, self-reports and cross-sectional modelling. Not a study of audit effectiveness, proof of causation or an ROI forecast. Do not misstate the result as “operational improvement has no business value.”

## B09 Inventory discrepancies and the work between counts

**Barratt, Mark, Thomas J. Kull and Anníbal Camara Sodero. (2018).** “Inventory record inaccuracy dynamics and the role of employees within multi-channel distribution center inventory systems.” *Journal of Operations Management*, 63(1), 6–24. [DOI 10.1016/j.jom.2018.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jom.2018.09.003); [ASU bibliographic record](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/inventory-record-inaccuracy-dynamics-and-the-role-of-employees-wi/); [author-uploaded manuscript](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328417219_Inventory_Record_Inaccuracy_Dynamics_and_the_Role_of_Employees_within_Multi-Channel_Distribution_Center_Inventory_Systems).

- **Type and role:** Academic fieldwork combining records, repeated counts, observation and interviews; particularly useful for inventory investigation and worker perspectives.
- **Passages:** Manuscript pp. 13–14, “Unit of Analysis and Data Sources” and “System Inventory Records and Inventory Counting”; pp. 16–17, “Documents, Interviews, and Observations”; abstract on daily variability.
- **Supports:** Examining activity between counts and how reporting discrepancies interacts with work measures and incentives. Worker accounts can expose a constraint that a balance correction misses.
- **Limits:** One retailer's distribution centre; selected items across ten business days; incoming quantities assumed correct. Keep observed findings separate from modelled effects and do not present the sampled behaviour as universal.

## B10 Blind counting with contextual alternatives

**United States General Accounting Office. (2002, March).** *Executive Guide: Best Practices in Achieving Consistent, Accurate Physical Counts of Inventory and Related Property*. GAO-02-447G. [Official PDF](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-02-447g.pdf).

- **Type and role:** Official practice guide informed by visits to 12 locations at seven selected companies; adds a procedural basis for the blind-count explanation.
- **Passages:** Key Factor 8, printed pp. 39–41, PDF pages 40–42; methodology, printed pp. 71–72. Keep printed and PDF pagination distinct.
- **Supports:** Counting without knowing/accessing the recorded quantity while retaining the identifying information needed for the work, followed by comparison and reconciliation.
- **Limits:** Dated, deliberately selected practice examples rather than representative prevalence or experimental evidence. The guide also describes non-blind counts with independent teams and compensating controls. It does not make blind counting universally mandatory or guarantee accuracy. No direct quotation is selected.

## B11 An HMRC-approved closing stock balance system

**HM Revenue & Customs. (2025, April 30).** “Duty management and closing stock balance systems.” *Special procedure: customs warehousing*. [Official guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/special-procedure-customs-warehousing/duty-management-and-closing-stock-balance-systems). Published and updated 30 April 2025; reopened 1 October 2026.

- **Type and role:** Official UK guidance; supports the approved closing-stock arrangement in the article and V04.
- **Passages:** Sections on use, approval and review of closing stock balance systems.
- **Supports:** Pre-removal commercial entries for consignment details, quantities and removal dates; duty-management updates as information becomes available, before midnight on the following warehouse operation day; an auditable link; both records jointly constituting the stock account.
- **Limits:** The draft's sequence concerns UK HMRC-approved closing stock balance systems. Approval conditions and applicable authorisation remain essential; this is neither a complete customs procedure nor permission for a particular release. No quotation is selected.

## Evidence still needed before detailed claims are written

- **Counting procedure:** B10 now supports the limited blind-count explanation. Any more detailed movement-control or count-programme instruction must stay within the relevant procedure and source. Avoid deterministic claims about what a tired worker will see.
- **Specialist detail:** B11 supplies the selected UK approved-system example. Further food, medicine, environmental or technical detail needs its own applicable source; do not merge those requirements into a generic compliance promise.
- **Charges and products:** use the relevant contract for billing entitlement. P01 verifies the narrow advertised carrier self-scheduling description; it does not verify quantified outcomes. Neither the bibliography nor a product mention establishes causation, savings or compliance.
- **Human input:** operator/domain review and authors' own experience can improve the manuscript. No interview, expert endorsement or first-hand experience is claimed on their behalf. Such input should be attributed only when actually obtained.
