## 2. Working position after this first pass

**The strongest baseline is to revamp the original audit page and use existing related pages where they already answer a different task.** A new generic assessment or generic downloadable audit-checklist page has not earned a separate role.

Two non-audit additions remain worth comparing: **daily/shift execution** and **physical-warehouse setup/launch**. Neither currently has demonstrated lead-generation value sufficient for the step 11 landing-page exception.

Picking and 5S have separately describable purposes, but that alone does not establish a worthwhile publishing project. A new safety page faces a more immediate objection: a dedicated safety checklist already exists. These are provisional judgements, not final exclusions.

## 3. Decision rules carried forward

An additional page needs all of the following, judged qualitatively rather than through a fabricated numerical score:

1. A materially different user task, decision or deliverable—not just a synonym, format or cadence modifier.
2. A clear contribution for relevant readers, including directors with indirect operational concerns.
3. Substantial useful content that existing pages cannot reasonably provide through a bounded section or onward link.
4. Enough evidence of likely benefit to justify effort and ongoing maintenance. Observed impressions are one input, not market size or a traffic forecast.
5. Explicit ownership and boundaries that avoid commissioning substantially duplicate answers.
6. A scope that preserves the audit page's investment priority.

The core audit page must keep useful inventory and general safety context. Specialist pages can provide deeper answers without stripping those subjects out of a general audit. Editorial scope can reduce deliberate duplication; it cannot guarantee which URL a search engine will show.
