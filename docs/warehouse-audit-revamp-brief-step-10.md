## 5. Checklist content model

Commission **28 starter checks across eight areas**, using the topic allocation below. These are drafting specifications, not yet validated technical instructions. Keep stable check identifiers across article, applet and files so a finding can be traced back to its question.

Every check needs: **the question being examined; the relevant criterion/reference to confirm; evidence to seek; and the next step if evidence is missing or a concern appears.** Examples of records are suggestions, not proof that a control works. Do not insert generic pass thresholds.

| Area / check IDs | Topics to turn into individual checks | Evidence and decision emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Inventory and records — INV01–03** | Physical quantity versus record; location/item/unit identity; discrepancy investigation and recurrence. | Sample/date/movement context, count records, transaction trail and explanations. Distinguish a matching balance from a reliable process. |
| **Receiving through dispatch — FLOW01–04** | Receipt identification and exceptions; putaway/staging handoff; pick/pack/dispatch verification; timing and resource coordination. | Trace selected transactions and exception handoffs. Compare arrival patterns, work mix and available resources without presuming scheduling is the cause. |
| **Layout, capacity and housekeeping — SPACE01–03** | Usable space and congestion; slotting/travel/replenishment; housekeeping ownership and recurring obstructions. | Observe relevant shifts and operating conditions; compare demand and usable capacity. Avoid a universal layout, aisle width or slotting prescription. |
| **Equipment and maintenance — EQUIP01–03** | Inspection/maintenance coverage; reported defects and restrictions; repair and return-to-use evidence. | Equipment identity, applicable inspection criteria and competent assessment. Do not tell an unqualified visitor to test or clear damaged equipment. |
| **People and workplace safety — PEOPLE01–03** | Site hazards and controls; competence and handover; workload, worker feedback and practical constraints. | Records plus observed work and worker accounts. Training completion alone does not establish effective control; productivity measures do not settle working-condition questions. |
| **Goods protection and specific requirements — GOODS01–04** | Access/custody/loss controls; product condition and traceability; controlled-goods or customs movement requirements where applicable; environmental/storage-condition obligations and exceptions. | Identify actual goods, activities, jurisdiction and customer/framework criteria. Explain the purpose and evidence chain, with precise domain examples only after verification. |
| **Financial controls and providers — CONTROL01–04** | Adjustment/write-off authority and supporting reasons; custody and reconciliation evidence for finance; contracted service/qualification evidence; charged activity, unit and rate evidence. | Separate control operation, financial conclusions, service decisions and invoice disputes. A variance is not proof of theft; an agreed invoice is not evidence that service or assurance requirements are met. |
| **Governance and follow-through — GOV01–04** | Defined purpose/criteria/responsibilities; evidence coverage and limitations; action ownership/resources/escalation; verification and recurring findings. | Make gaps in authority and evidence visible. A completed action is not automatically effective, and an overall favourable result cannot cancel an unresolved consequential finding. |

Avoid an unreadable desktop-width spreadsheet in the article. Use eight small groups with a consistent check/evidence layout that becomes stacked content on narrow screens. The workbook can support wider working tables. The applet may select a subset; the full public checklist remains available.

## 6. Required depth for financial, compliance and governance readers

### Financial and inventory-control explanation

Give this topic a real explanatory section. Show the chain from a physical observation to the transaction record, discrepancy explanation, authorised adjustment, reconciliation and review. Explain the different questions of quantity, condition, custody, ownership and valuation without pretending that one operational count answers them all.

Correct the current page's oversimplified operational/financial split. Financial auditors can examine physical quantities and condition; they do not merely value stock. PCAOB AS 2510 provides a scoped US public-company auditing example and expressly treats inventory observation as only part of the necessary work. It is a source for this distinction, not a universal warehouse-audit method. [PCAOB AS 2510](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2510)

Use an attributed field example if helpful: Martin County's 2021 utilities warehouse audit examined weak documentation for inventory adjustments and whether earlier corrective work could actually be evaluated. The case makes evidence and follow-through tangible. Keep its public-sector context, historical date and selected sample visible; do not transfer its frequencies, tolerances or findings to every warehouse. [Primary audit report](https://www.martinclerk.com/DocumentCenter/View/856/Utilities-Warehouse-Audit-Report---2021-PDF)

Give warehouse charges a distinct short worked chain: applicable contract/rate version → charged event or unit → source record → discrepancy → responsible commercial decision. Do not infer contract entitlement or calculate hypothetical savings from a missing record. This is more useful than merging billing into stock valuation.

### Requirements and assurance explanation

Help readers establish what the review seeks to demonstrate, to whom, and under which criteria. Distinguish internal procedure, customer contract, voluntary framework and law. Explain that criteria may already be imposed by the engagement, rather than selected at the sponsor's discretion.

Show how to assemble relevant procedures, transaction/condition records, exceptions, prior findings and corrective evidence, with a responsible owner for each. Identify what missing or contradictory evidence means for the review; a document's existence is not a favourable finding.

A substantive specialist example should explain the objective, evidence and management consequence. It can go beyond a signpost, provided any actual technical prescription is supported by current, applicable sources and suitable review. Do not fill the page with unsupported technical rules or with repeated disclaimers.

Operator evidence supports this mixed reality: United Warehouse describes recurring internal, third-party, customer and insurance-related reviews. This is an operator's account of its own activity, not independent verification or a cadence to copy. [United Warehouse's audit account](https://www.unitedwarehouse.com/why-united/quality-assurance/audits/)

### Governance and decision explanation

Specify the responsibilities to establish: sponsor/decision owner, evidence providers, assessor, action owner, resource approver and effectiveness reviewer. One person may hold several roles; acknowledge the resulting limits instead of inventing a large organisation or implying independent review.

Explain escalation when the team cannot obtain records, agree criteria, fund corrective work or resolve a cross-company dependency. A director should receive the scope, key findings, confidence/limitations, consequences, options, resource decisions and verification commitments—not simply a red/amber/green site score.

Keep business outcomes separate from operating improvements. The prior academic evidence found an indirect relationship between warehouse waste reduction and business performance through operational/distribution performance in its studied population. It does not support a universal profit or ROI claim. [Abushaikha, Salhieh and Towers, 2018](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5801/)

## 7. Worked examples to commission

These are **hypothetical teaching examples**, not DataDocks customer results. Publish the assumptions and uncertain conclusions with each.

### Example A: a repeated inventory discrepancy

Carry one finding all the way through: selected bin/item count differs from the recorded quantity; record the count/movement conditions and examined records; distinguish the immediate correction from investigation; compare plausible unit-of-measure, transaction timing, location and handling explanations; identify what evidence would discriminate between them.

Then show authorised record correction, an accountable process action and a later effectiveness check under relevant operating conditions. The initial variance does not establish fraud, a software defect or a dock problem. If a cause remains unconfirmed, the action is further investigation rather than a fabricated diagnosis.

The final example should appear in the article, applet demonstration and pack with the same IDs and labels. It must show at least one unresolved point, so the output does not imply every review ends with certainty.

### Example B: a director's mixed 3PL renewal

Use late dispatch, a product-storage concern and disputed charges as three connected issues. Show the service commitment and timestamp evidence; the product/customer requirement and appropriate quality assessment; and the rate/activity records for the invoice question. Give each its own criteria, evidence limits and responsible decision owner.

The management conclusion can be an evidence request, funded corrective plan, further specialist assessment or a commercial decision. A favourable certificate does not resolve dispatch performance; a credited invoice does not resolve the product concern. Do not manufacture a single renewal recommendation from incomplete facts.

Together these examples demonstrate operational, financial, compliance and governance depth without turning the pillar into a universal technical manual.
