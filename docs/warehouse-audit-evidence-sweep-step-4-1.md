# Warehouse audit: evidence sweep — step 4.1

Research date: 28 September 2026. Status: step 4.1 complete. This is an additive review of [steps 3.1 and 4](warehouse-audit-groups-and-user-needs-steps-3-1-4.md), preserving the earlier analysis and L01–L18 identifiers.

## Purpose, method and limits

The previous evidence leaned toward software suppliers, consultants and official guidance. This sweep sought academic work that could complicate that picture and operator accounts that could show how audits, checks and corrective actions work in practice. Operator experience remains especially valuable; academic publication alone does not make a source operator-grounded.

Searches covered warehouse assessment, inventory discrepancies, management practices, safety, 5S, pharmaceutical storage, audit preparation and corrective-action follow-through. We favoured original field studies, accessible methods, operator-published accounts and concrete practitioner discussions. Related studies were eligible even if they did not study use of the exact phrase “warehouse audit”; that distinction is recorded below. This was a targeted evidence sweep, not a systematic literature review or a representative operator survey.

All DataDocks-owned and DataDocks-authored material remained excluded, including externally hosted contributions. Neither the yard-management comparison nor the existing audit page or search-performance data was used.

The retained set comprises eight academic sources, one institutional field audit, three operator-company accounts and two anonymous practitioner discussions. These categories overlap in evidential value: an academic researcher embedded in a warehouse can supply more observable workplace context than a corporate success story. The sweep did not produce independently verified, named firsthand accounts across all eighteen meanings.

All links below were assessed on 28 September 2026. Findings are separated from our implications. None establishes query frequency, searcher identity, market size or DataDocks conversion potential.

## Evidence register

### A01 — A warehouse assessment combines observation and conversation

René de Koster, *Warehouse Assessment in a Single Tour*: [university repository, 2012 chapter](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/84570/); [accessible manuscript](https://www.eur.nl/en/erim/media/2025-08-warehouseassessmentacad). The manuscript describes an earlier version of the method; do not confuse manuscript and publication dates.

- **Evidence/access:** Full manuscript; tour-based assessment and limited validation with participating visitors and managers.
- **Finding:** The method combines observation with questions for workers and managers. Inbound/outbound coordination is one part of a broader assessment. Recognised weaknesses can persist because managers lack authority or resources to fix them.
- **Implication:** L01 needs explanation, prioritisation and escalation as well as scoring.
- **Limit:** An older, approximate assessment method, not a universal benchmark or proof that a brief tour establishes compliance.

### A02 — Operational improvement and business results are different outcomes

Abushaikha, Salhieh and Towers (2018), *Improving distribution and business performance through lean warehousing*: [university record](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5801/), [accepted paper](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/5801/1/5801%20Towers%20%282018%29%20Improving%20distribution%20and%20business%20performance%20through%20lean%20warehousing.pdf).

- **Evidence/access:** Full paper; Delphi work and 270 survey responses aggregated across 90 firms in five Middle Eastern countries, including logistics, warehouse/distribution and general managers.
- **Finding:** Waste reduction was associated with operational/distribution performance; the model did not establish a direct relationship with business performance.
- **Implication:** L01 users need a reasoned link between operating measures and business outcomes.
- **Limit:** Regional, self-reported, cross-sectional evidence. Associations and modelled mediation do not prove a specific intervention's financial return.

### A03 — Inventory errors are generated between counts

Barratt, Kull and Sodero (2018), *Inventory record inaccuracy dynamics and the role of employees within multi-channel distribution center inventory systems*: [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jom.2018.09.003), [author-uploaded paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328417219_Inventory_Record_Inaccuracy_Dynamics_and_the_Role_of_Employees_within_Multi-Channel_Distribution_Center_Inventory_Systems).

- **Evidence/access:** Full text; one US apparel distribution centre, a short observation window for selected SKUs, operational records, observation/interviews and a system-dynamics model.
- **Finding:** Periodic accuracy measures can obscure daily discrepancy dynamics. Reporting shortages can impose time and incentive costs on workers.
- **Implication:** L02/L15 should include how discrepancies arise and are reported, not only how stock is counted.
- **Limit:** Single-site field evidence; simulation results are distinct from observed outcomes. Receipts were assumed correct in the study's scope.

### A04 — Warehouse management is contingent on the operation

Faber, de Koster and Smidts (2013), *Organizing warehouse management*: [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1108/IJOPM-12-2011-0471), [accessible paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/263590784_Organizing_warehouse_management).

- **Evidence/access:** Full text; survey of 215 warehouses in the Netherlands and Flanders, predominantly senior warehouse-manager respondents.
- **Finding:** Task complexity was associated with planning, decision-rule complexity, control sophistication and WMS specificity. Production and distribution warehouses differed in how assortment changes related to planning extensiveness.
- **Implication:** L01/L15 need operating context before prescribing a supposedly universal level of process or systems sophistication.
- **Limit:** Older regional, cross-sectional, single-informant survey. It does not measure modern software suitability or director search behaviour.

### A05 — Recorded productivity needs a worker-level explanation

Ruben Timmerman (2026), *We are all replaceable: Workplace control and (digitalised) surveillance in logistics warehouses*: [journal article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10611-026-10296-z).

- **Evidence/access:** Full article; three months of covert agency work across three Dutch warehouses, within a wider research programme.
- **Finding:** Workers encounter delays associated with equipment, congestion, replenishment and order characteristics alongside productivity pressure.
- **Implication:** L01/L04/L15/L18 need worker accounts and constraints when interpreting recorded performance or stated procedures.
- **Limit:** Contextual, qualitative scholarship with a critical lens, not a prevalence estimate or a study of formal audit effectiveness. Researcher participation supplies workplace experience, not direct testimony from every affected worker.

### A06 — Small sites concentrate management and implementation responsibilities

Carmo Neto and Longhini (2024), *Implementation of the 5S program in a civil construction material warehouse*: [DOI](https://doi.org/10.47456/bjpe.v10i2.44234), [full Portuguese paper](https://periodicos.ufes.br/bjpe/article/download/44234/30265).

- **Evidence/access:** Full paper; single-site qualitative internship/action case in a small Brazilian building-materials business, using observations, photographs and interviews.
- **Finding:** Storage identification, obstructed movement and an owner with multiple management responsibilities were part of the improvement context; the researcher conducted the diagnosis. Implementation included education and ongoing responsibilities.
- **Implication:** L06/L02 guidance should work when several organisational roles belong to one person.
- **Limit:** Mixed retail/depot/workshop setting; no controlled evidence of transferable productivity gains.

### A07 — Safety priorities can differ by organisational level

Hofstra, Petkova, Dullaert, Reniers and de Leeuw (2018), *Assessing and facilitating warehouse safety*: [accepted manuscript](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/33025/1/PubSub10225_De-Leeuw.pdf).

- **Evidence/access:** Full manuscript; three sites of one Dutch logistics service provider, with site visits, interviews, ratings and conversations involving managers, team leaders and workers.
- **Finding:** Perceptions of safety and task priorities differed across organisational levels; team leaders matter to workers' experience of safety leadership.
- **Implication:** L04/L12 need examination of everyday implementation as well as management commitments.
- **Limit:** Exploratory single-company study. Inclusion of hazardous-goods operations does not make this a complete dangerous-goods compliance study.

### A08 — Documentation cannot compensate for physical constraints

Boche, Mulugeta and Gudeta (2020), *Assessment of Inventory Management Practices at Ethiopian Pharmaceuticals Supply Agency, Addis Ababa, Ethiopia*: [open-access record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7548323/).

- **Evidence/access:** Abstract and indexed open-access text; direct access encountered a verification screen. Cross-sectional study of ten central-agency warehouses, including observation, record review and seventeen interviews.
- **Finding:** Space, older facilities, equipment and information constraints affected inventory/storage practice.
- **Implication:** L11/L02/L01 should distinguish missing paperwork from conditions requiring physical or organisational investment.
- **Limit:** One public agency and resource context; not a general GDP certification assessment. Full-text access was incomplete, so conclusions remain narrow.

### F01 — Repeated inspections do not ensure durable correction

USDA Office of Inspector General (2019), *AMS Storage and Handling of Commodities for International Food Assistance Programs*, audit 01601-0002-41: [primary report](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2020-05/01601-0002-41.pdf).

- **Evidence/access:** Full report; eight non-statistically selected licensed operators, site visits, interviews and review of examination reports.
- **Finding:** Repeated sanitation/pest findings coexisted with weaknesses in expectations and corrective-action tracking.
- **Implication:** L10/L14/L06 need evidence that findings were resolved and stayed resolved, not merely that another inspection occurred.
- **Limit:** Institutional evidence, retained for its fieldwork. A particular food-assistance storage programme does not represent commodity finance, customs or all food warehouses.

### O01 — Audit work includes coordinating reviewers and follow-up

Malcolm Group (December 2025), *24seven*, issue 18, warehouse/compliance account, printed pages 10–11: [operator magazine](https://www.malcolmgroup.co.uk/media/2954/5310-24seven-issue-18-final_print_lr-11-12-25.pdf).

- **Evidence/access:** Full relevant spread; operator-published account with Warehouse and Compliance Support contributor Paul Walton identified.
- **Finding:** The account describes several sites, new BRCGS auditors, customer audits and responses to minor findings.
- **Implication:** L07/L10 also involve audit scheduling, explaining practices to different assessors and allocating follow-up responsibility.
- **Limit:** A celebratory corporate account, not independently inspected audit records. Audit scheduling here is administrative coordination, not evidence of dock-appointment product fit.

### O02 — Customer assurance is an ongoing activity

United Warehouse, *Warehouse Facility Audits*, undated: [operator account](https://www.unitedwarehouse.com/why-united/quality-assurance/audits/).

- **Evidence/access:** Full page; the operator describes monthly GMP self-audits, external reviews and audits by customers and their customers.
- **Finding:** Several assessment relationships can coexist in ordinary service delivery.
- **Implication:** L07 should include recurring assurance work; a customer audit does not necessarily indicate a failed relationship or a new procurement exercise.
- **Limit:** Self-described corporate practice with no named individual or audit outcomes. Commercial material remains commercial even when the publisher runs warehouses.

### O03 — Inventory discovery speed matters during live operations

ODW Logistics (31 July 2026), *How ODW Prevented 90% of Inventory Discrepancies*: [operator-published case](https://www.odwlogistics.com/resources/how-odw-prevented-90-of-inventory-discrepancies).

- **Evidence/access:** Full page; an operator's account of a technology partnership.
- **Finding:** It contrasts lengthy warehouse audits with identifying discrepancies before picking.
- **Implication:** L02 includes discovery and resolution during ongoing operations, not just periodic assurance.
- **Limit:** Vendor-associated promotional evidence. The headline's “prevented” differs from the body's measure of discrepancies identified before picking; the percentage is not adopted as a verified prevention result or product recommendation.

### O04 — Counting and correcting the source of errors are separate jobs

Reddit, *Cycle Counts*, 15 August 2025: [practitioner discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Warehouseworkers/comments/1mqtui2/cycle_counts/).

- **Evidence/access:** Thread text; anonymous self-reports. The original poster describes counting bins and submitting results to a supervisor, with errors recurring. Other participants describe investigation responsibilities and system/workflow obstacles.
- **Implication:** L02 should distinguish counter, approver, investigator and process owner; the same person need not perform all four roles.
- **Limit:** Roles and accounts are unverified. Replies are separate accounts, not corroboration of the original site's causes. No inference about theft, prevalence or a particular system's failure is warranted.

### O05 — Repeated training may leave the constraint untouched

IFSQN (13 August 2020), *Staff not implementing corrective actions from an audit nonconformance*: [practitioner discussion](https://www.ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/39144-staff-not-implementing-corrective-actions-from-an-audit-nonconformance/).

- **Evidence/access:** Search-indexed text reproducing the original post and some replies; the live thread was inaccessible, including a 403 on reload. Pseudonymous account, job title not verified. This was not a full live-thread review.
- **Finding:** The poster reports repeated packaging-protection problems in a warehouse despite training, with workload and disputed responsibility entering the discussion.
- **Implication:** L10 corrective-action education should ask about resources, ownership and sustained verification as well as training.
- **Limit:** One older, unverified food-sector account. It raises explanations to investigate; it does not establish the cause of recurrence.

## What changes in step 4

The sweep strengthens and qualifies the earlier user-needs analysis. It does **not** justify another meaning of the phrase, a wholesale regrouping, or claims that any meaning dominates searches.

| Earlier emphasis | Addition or correction | Principal basis |
|---|---|---|
| Define scope, collect evidence and produce findings. | Explain what happens between discovering a problem and securing a durable improvement: investigation, authority, resources, ownership and verification. | A01, F01, O05 |
| Inventory users need accurate counts and records. | Separate detection, record correction and prevention. Investigate how normal work and incentives create discrepancies; a completed recount is not the whole outcome. | A03, O03, O04 |
| Managers, assessors and sponsors have different needs. | Add team leaders and workers as evidence sources and action owners; in a small site, one person may hold several roles. | A05, A06, A07 |
| A performance audit helps prioritise improvement. | Make site context and alternative explanations explicit. A score or apparent productivity difference can hide a resource or workflow constraint. | A04, A05, A08 |
| A sponsor wants value from improvement. | Distinguish operational measures from wider business results. A plausible business case still requires evidence connecting the two. | A02 |
| Third-party audits support qualification or performance review. | Include recurring, overlapping customer assurance and the burden of hosting different reviewers, even in stable relationships. | O01, O02 |
| Compliance needs evidence of adherence and corrective action. | Check ordinary work and repeated findings, not only the documented procedure or the most recent closed action. | A07, F01, O05 |

These are additions to educational needs, not proof that searchers request each one. “Control” also needs context: managing work through planning and feedback is not automatically a separate governance audit.

The user's clarified target audience includes logistics and supply-chain directors whose pains may be indirect. That is a **user-provided strategic direction**, not a finding from these studies. The evidence makes room for that perspective by showing organisational constraints, differing management levels and the distinction between operational and business results. It does not prove that directors search the phrase or that DataDocks resolves the underlying problems.

Searches also found adjacent retail food-audit research, including [Traversa et al. (2022)](https://www.pagepressjournals.org/index.php/ijfs/article/download/10022/10505). It was not used as warehouse corroboration because its observed settings were retail businesses. Excluding a superficially relevant study is preferable to silently generalising its population.

No precise priority ranking follows from the number of sources attached to a meaning. Uneven coverage reflects this sweep, not commercial opportunity. The strongest triangulation here combines academic fieldwork with concrete operator accounts; anonymous discussions remain leads for investigation, and corporate accounts remain interested testimony.
