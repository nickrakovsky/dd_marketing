# Warehouse Audit Checklist

*Working manuscript for Post 1, 30 September 2026. Drafting turn 1 supplies the opening, skeleton and initial topic bullets. These are not yet the finished checklist or publishable article. Source numbers refer to the [working bibliography](warehouse-audit-article-bibliography.md). Editorial notes at the end are outside the article.*

Operating a warehouse requires managing the constant tension between speed, safety, and accuracy. A warehouse audit compares what happens on the floor and in the records with the requirements the operation is meant to meet. This checklist helps you plan that review, investigate discrepancies and put the findings in front of someone who can act.

## What is a warehouse audit

A warehouse audit is a structured review of a physical warehouse's activities, records and controls against defined requirements. Its scope depends on the question being asked: whether inventory records are reliable, whether goods are handled as required, whether charges are supported, or whether corrective work has actually solved a problem.

- Distinguish an audit from a daily task list, a stock count and a new-site setup plan.
- Explain how an internal review, customer review and specialist assurance engagement can examine related work for different purposes.
- Give readers a short route to the checklist or to planning the review they need.

*Source leads: B01, B06 and B07. This is a working explanation, not a quotation from a standard.*

## Plan the audit around the decision you need to make

- Start with the problem or decision: recurring errors, service failures, a customer requirement, provider renewal or a proposed investment.
- Define the site, period, activities, goods and criteria; identify what the review excludes.
- Separate the person commissioning the review, the people supplying evidence, the assessor and the people who can approve action or resources.
- Give a director without a diagnosis a useful evidence request; give the operating team a manageable preparation task.

*Source leads: B01, B05 and B07. Role allocation is our proposed method, to be adapted to the organisation.*

## Executing the Audit Don’t Boil the Ocean

### Follow the records and watch the work

- Combine selected transaction records, observation and conversations with the people doing the work.
- Identify representative operating conditions and record the limits of the sample.
- Keep missing or conflicting evidence visible.

### Keep stock counts and process observation distinct

- Explain count boundaries, movement records and recounts.
- Develop the blind-counting topic only after checking an appropriate procedural source.
- Preserve ordinary working conditions when examining how the process works; avoid preparing away the problem under review.

*Source leads: B01, B02, B05 and B09.*

## Stock discrepancies financial controls and warehouse charges

### Follow a discrepancy through the records

- Physical observation → transaction history → possible explanation → authorised adjustment → reconciliation and review.
- Separate quantity, condition, custody, ownership and valuation questions.
- Use the Martin County report as an attributed, bounded example of adjustment documentation and follow-up.

### Check the basis of a warehouse charge

- Applicable contract/rate version → charged event or unit → supporting record → discrepancy → commercial decision owner.
- Keep billing, service performance and stock valuation as distinct questions within the same provider relationship.

*Source leads: B05 and B06. The billing chain is an editorial method; contract-specific claims require the actual applicable terms.*

## Establish the requirements for compliance and customer audits

- Separate internal procedures, customer contracts, voluntary frameworks and legal requirements.
- Establish whose assessment is required, what competence is needed and which evidence each owner must supply.
- Explain what a missing record or unresolved requirement means for the review.
- Use one warehouse operator's account to show how internal, third-party, customer and insurer reviews coexist.
- Develop a specific domain example only with the applicable source and context; retain substantive explanation of evidence and responsibilities.

*Source leads: B04 and B07. The bibliography identifies where further domain-specific evidence is needed.*

## Turn findings into decisions and follow-through

### Dig for the Root Cause

- Preserve the practical distinction between correcting today's stock balance and investigating recurrence.
- Keep similar-looking items and changed case packs as possible explanations to test, alongside transaction timing and location errors.
- Let an unresolved cause lead to an evidence request rather than a confident diagnosis.

### Give each action an owner and a route to a decision

- Record the finding, evidence, consequence, action, owner, resources and target date.
- Make a blocked decision visible when the action owner lacks authority or resources.
- Give a director the scope, consequential findings, uncertainty, options and decisions needed.

### Check whether the fix worked

- Distinguish action completion from evidence that the problem has been addressed.
- Separate an operational improvement from any claimed financial or wider business result.

### Worked example of a repeated inventory discrepancy

- Carry one explicitly hypothetical finding through evidence, competing explanations, action and planned verification; leave one point honestly unresolved.

### Worked example of a mixed warehouse provider review

- Use an explicitly hypothetical 3PL renewal involving late dispatch, a storage concern and disputed charges; keep criteria, owners and conclusions distinct.

*Source leads: B01, B05, B08 and B09. These sources inform the method; the two teaching examples are not reported customer cases.*

## How often should you audit a warehouse

- Distinguish routine/pre-use checks, cycle counts, broader process reviews and specified assurance engagements.
- Explain how risk, change, previous findings and applicable requirements affect cadence.
- Avoid a universal monthly or quarterly rule; keep any sourced frequency attached to its actual setting.

*Source leads: B04, B06 and B07. They illustrate different review contexts, not a common schedule.*

## How Technology Should Help

- Assess whether paper records, spreadsheets, scanners or warehouse systems help the team collect and reconcile the evidence it needs.
- Explain what photographs can record and what still needs competent interpretation or corroboration.
- Discuss appointment/arrival coordination only where the evidence points to that problem; verify retained DataDocks capability claims before drafting them.

*Source leads: B02, B03 and B09. Product claims need separate verification. The public planner, pack, images and CTA placement will be integrated in turn 2; this skeleton does not settle their locations.*
