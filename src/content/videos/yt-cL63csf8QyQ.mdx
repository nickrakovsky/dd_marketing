## Dock Congestion: Why Your Warehouse Backs Up by Noon

Dock congestion usually doesn’t start when you see trucks backed up at noon. It starts earlier, when too many arrivals hit the same part of the schedule and your team spends the rest of the shift trying to catch up.

One late truck turns into three trucks waiting. Doors stay tied up longer than planned. Drivers start looking for somewhere to go. And before long, your team is moving people around just to deal with whatever is in front of them.

In this video, we break down the problems that create those midday backups—and practical ways to keep your docks and yard moving before the rush gets out of hand.

You’ll learn:

✅ Why your intake rules can create a rush when too many trucks are competing for the same doors and labor
✅ How better warehouse dock scheduling can spread arrivals around the capacity your team actually has
✅ What to do when a driver arrives and their planned door isn’t available instead of letting the truck sit and block traffic
✅ Why leaving too much “buffer” in the wrong places can actually make peak periods harder to manage
✅ How clearer ownership on the floor can keep every small delay from turning into another problem the warehouse manager has to solve personally
