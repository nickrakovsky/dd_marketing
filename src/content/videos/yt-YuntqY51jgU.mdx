## The two ways new warehouse managers fail: hiding in your office or doing your supervisors' jobs.

Newly promoted warehouse managers often struggle to strike the right balance between strategic office work and floor leadership. Trying to do all the physical floor work yourself prevents you from coaching shift supervisors, while hiding in the office working on executive reports disconnects you from daily warehouse operations.

Successful operations management requires a daily balancing act. Your primary role as a manager is coaching supervisors, providing necessary operational tools, and guiding team decision-making. Spending time on the warehouse floor every day maintains a strong leadership presence, ensures processes are running smoothly, and keeps you connected to your frontline workers.

This video covers new warehouse manager tips, balancing floor presence vs office work, coaching shift supervisors, warehouse leadership strategy, distribution center operations, and avoiding management burnout.

### Key Takeaways

- **Operational Focus:** Real-time visibility and structured dock scheduling eliminate carrier congestion and turn dock bottlenecks into predictable flows.
- **Carrier Communication:** Digital driver check-in and automated notifications keep facility teams aligned and prepared ahead of truck arrivals.
- **Facility Throughput:** Consistent appointment scheduling prevents detention fees and maximizes dock utilization.
