## Fix Warehouse Exceptions Before They Back Up the Dock

If your warehouse exception handling falls apart every time a damaged or shifted load shows up, one bad truck can tie up a dock door and throw off everything scheduled behind it.

The freight still has to go somewhere. Somebody has to document what happened. Your receiving team needs to know whether to unload it, hold it, reject it, or move it somewhere it won’t block the rest of the operation.

In this video, we break down a practical workflow for handling inbound warehouse exceptions without letting one problem load turn into an all-day backup.

You’ll learn:

✅ How to separate different types of receiving exceptions so your team knows what needs immediate attention and what can be moved aside
✅ How to document damaged freight, missing paperwork, and other problems without tying up the dock longer than necessary
✅ How to move a problem load out of the main flow so one exception doesn’t create dock congestion for every truck behind it
✅ How clearer expectations with customers, suppliers, and carriers can reduce repeat problems before the freight reaches your facility
✅ How a consistent exception process gives your team a clear next step instead of waiting for the warehouse manager to make every call
