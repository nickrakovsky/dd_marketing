Suppose an inspection pauses while the receiver walks to a desk for the checklist. The check is required, but the trip to collect it has become part of how the job gets done. Watching the task closely gives a manager something specific to improve.

DataDocks creator and CEO Nick Rakovsky recommends starting with one area of the warehouse and spending time with the people doing the work. His question is practical: what would make that person's job easier?

In the video below (10:56), Nick explains how to turn those observations into a process the team can use.

## Watch the task before choosing the fix

Nick uses quality checks as an example. Where does the receiver stop? What do they need to get off the forklift to do? Are the materials laid out so they can complete the inspection easily?

A quick walk past may miss those details. Choose one activity and watch enough of it to understand the sequence, including what happens before and after it. Ask the team what slows them down and have them show you. The difficulty might come from the layout, the available equipment, or a step that made sense when the procedure was written but works poorly now.

Nick also cautions against arriving with a favourite solution. A change has to work with the people, materials, and space on the floor. Their experience should help shape the trial.

## Test one change with the team

In the inspection example, the team could try keeping the current checklist at the inspection point. The inspection itself stays the same.

That is a small trial with a clear question: can the receiver complete the same check without leaving the work area to collect the form? Watch whether it removes the interruption and whether the new arrangement creates any other difficulty.

Choose a trial period that gives the team enough comparable work to judge it. Record how the task works today and decide what to measure before making the change. For this example, that could include trips to fetch paperwork and the time needed to complete the inspection. Check that the required inspection records are still complete.

## Give someone responsibility for what happens next

Nick recommends putting a supervisor or team lead in charge of the trial. They can see how it works during the shift, gather feedback, and bring problems back while the details are fresh.

If the change helps, update the procedure so other shifts and new starters can follow it. Keep that person involved after rollout: the job may change as volumes, products, or the layout change. If the trial falls short, use what the team observed to revise it before extending it across the warehouse.
