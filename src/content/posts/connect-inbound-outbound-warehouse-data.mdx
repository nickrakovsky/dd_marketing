When I think about where technology could improve warehouse operations, one opportunity is connecting the information behind the movements happening throughout the day. What's arriving, where space is available, what needs preparing and what's due to leave all affect how work should be sequenced.

I see a similar opportunity inside the warehouse to the way a TMS helps coordinate a driver's work. Goods at the back need bringing forward to prepare a known outbound load. An incoming truck needs unloading. Information about both jobs can influence when those movements should happen.

Suppose an inbound load is due, but a suitable storage location is still occupied by goods that are ready for dispatch. There’s room for those goods in the dispatch staging area. If they are still in storage when the inbound load arrives, the team may have to unload into a temporary location and move the incoming goods again later.

Knowing the arrival time and outbound requirement gives the team another option: bring the outbound stock forward first, then put the incoming goods into the freed location after receipt checks. The space still needs to be confirmed available and suitable. The difference is that both requirements informed the sequence before the work was assigned.

That is one example of what connected information can make possible. Which sequence works depends on the load, the available space and what else is happening on that shift.

The information on arrivals, available space and outbound orders has to be reliable. The sequence also has to reflect what the team can actually get done on the floor. When conditions change, the plan needs to change with them.

But I think this is where the opportunity is: bringing inbound, space and outbound information together so the team can coordinate work that would otherwise be planned separately. That gives technology a practical way to help reduce unnecessary warehouse travel.
