A lot of logistics depends on people knowing when to give a little. There is a history between businesses: what they expect from each other, what they've worked through together and how they've handled problems in the past.

As more operational work becomes automated, I think we have to be careful about losing that context. A system can deal with a transaction without recognising what its response will mean to the person receiving it.

Take a hypothetical example. Goods arrive damaged, and a system proposes a charge to the customer. It has identified the damage and applied the instruction it was given. From that transaction alone, the response looks straightforward.

The customer may see it differently. They send substantial volumes through the operation, problems are rare, and they expect some give-and-take. They may accept that the damage occurred and still be frustrated by how it was handled.

That reaction is part of the decision. It doesn't disappear because a system has calculated an amount.

This is where I would want someone who understands the relationship to review the proposed response. What has been agreed? How have similar situations been handled? Does this need a conversation before anything goes out?

The decision might still be to apply the charge. The point is to reach that decision with an understanding of the business on the other side. A conversation can establish expectations, explain the response and give the other person a chance to raise something the system hasn't considered.

I can see technology doing more of the work involved in finding a problem and bringing the relevant information together. That could leave operations teams spending less time chasing emails and more time dealing with the exceptions that need their attention.

But those exceptions will still involve people. Optimising each individual transaction for the most immediate return can create friction across a relationship that has taken years to build.

When deciding what to automate, leave room for the judgment that keeps businesses willing to work together.
