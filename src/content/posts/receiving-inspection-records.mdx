By the time a client sends a photograph of a crushed carton, the pallet has been broken down and the delivery driver has gone. The warehouse still has the signed receiving sheet. Beside the word “inspected” are the receiver’s initials. The account manager can establish who worked the load. It’s harder to tell the client which cartons that employee could see, whether the damage was visible on arrival or what happened to the affected goods.

For a 3PL selling receiving services, those details carry commercial weight. They establish what the warehouse found when it took custody and how it performed the checks the client bought. Three crushed cartons on an identified pallet give an account manager a manageable claim to investigate. A vague record can leave an entire receipt in dispute, including work the team carried out correctly.

## Give the crew the inspection the client actually bought

Start with one account’s statement of work and an incoming load. Which surfaces must the receiver examine? Which loads need a temperature reading? Who can decide what to do if goods arrive damaged? A floor lead who can answer those questions can put a short set of instructions on the receiving sheet that will work on either shift.

In its agreement with Prestige Brands, GEODIS undertook to examine the exterior of every inbound shipment for mold, mildew and significant damage. It also agreed to inspect trailers, take temperature readings on refrigerated loads and keep a record covering all required inbound inspections. The same agreement recognized that packaging or pallet configuration might conceal an issue until later. In those cases, GEODIS would notify the client promptly after discovery.<Cite id="1" title="Master Logistics Services Agreement" citation="Prestige Brands and GEODIS Logistics, 2019" /> That detail matters when someone asks what a receiver could have seen before putaway.

Once a pallet has been split for picking, the claims team may have only the receiving record and the carton number the client supplies.

## Follow the affected units through the handoff

Cargo surveyor Yasar Tufail explained how to document a port handoff in *The Supply Chainer*. He recommends that the teams releasing and accepting a load check its identifying marks and visible condition together, then keep the photographs with a record of where and when they inspected it and who acknowledged it.<Cite id="2" title="Cargo Damage Claims Can Turn on One Photo at the Quay" citation="Hernandez, 2026" /> If a driver and receiver both see a crushed corner before a pallet comes off the truck, ask them to acknowledge the pallet number and the damage on the receipt. The claims team can then tell what both people saw while the freight was still on the truck.

At unloading, record how many cartons staff examined and when. Take one photograph that ties the affected cartons to the pallet label and a close-up of the damaged edges. Record where the team placed them and who received the exception notice. If staff only examined the outer packaging, say so. The client may still need to open a carton to decide what to do with the goods inside.

The client may have a decision to make before anyone moves the stock. Tarigan and colleagues observed an Indonesian 3PL whose warehouse forwarded a receipt report to its contract logistics team after a successful inspection. When the warehouse found damage, it sought the customer’s instruction. Goods could go back to the supplier or be repackaged depending on that response.<Cite id="3" title="Analysis of Delivery Delays Using Statistical Process Control at 3PL Company" citation="Tarigan et al., 2024" /> For your own account, attach the instruction to the same receipt as the observation. That gives the next shift a basis for holding, returning or releasing those particular units.

## Check what the record can answer

There may be a real cost to finding the right evidence a day late. Cargill’s manual instructs its third-party warehouses to identify and photograph damage upon receipt. It warns that notifying Cargill after the receipt date may leave the warehouse bearing the expense.<Cite id="4" title="Third-party warehouse and distribution manual" citation="Cargill, 2023" /> At a site serving that client, a photograph taken on Friday and passed to the account team on Monday raises two questions with different answers. When was the condition first observed, and when did the client learn of it?

Look at coverage across the account as well as the detail on one claim. Suppose 20 receipts last week required this inspection and 18 have a linked, completed record. Documented coverage is 90%. Pull the two outstanding receipts while the shifts can still explain them. One receiver might have filed a signed sheet under the wrong purchase order. The other receipt might have gone straight to putaway with the check outstanding. The first calls for finding and linking a record. The second calls for investigating the missed inspection and its consequences for that shipment.

Ask a lead from another shift to read one of the 18 completed reports. Can they identify the load, the inspection it received and exactly which cartons were affected? A team can reach its coverage target and still need to improve the descriptions its claims staff depend on.

## Make completion part of closing the receipt

A paper process can handle this. Put the client’s required checks on the sheet that follows its freight to the door. Before closing the receipt, the lead matches the sheet and any photographs to the receipt number, then checks for an owner on every open exception. Record any later observation with its own date so the account manager can distinguish what staff saw at unloading from what they found in storage.

The crew can record quantities, enter observations and attach photos at the door. The completed report can be exported and sent to the client. The closing rule addresses a specific point of failure, when someone is about to finish the booking and the client’s inspection record is still blank.

When the client’s photograph arrives, the account manager can start with the carton in question, trace where it went after inspection and check who authorized its release or return. The warehouse can account for the service it performed even if the cause of the damage is still under investigation.
