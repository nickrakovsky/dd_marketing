You asked a supervisor to get the overflow bay ready for the next delivery. Later, the pallets are still there. They have been working through stock checks you also asked them to finish. You were expecting the bay cleared by mid-afternoon; they planned to do it before the shift ended.

Before deciding they chose the wrong priority, check whether they knew when the space was needed.

DataDocks creator and CEO Nick Rakovsky makes this point when discussing the move from supervisor to warehouse manager. Giving someone a specific completion date, he explains, gives them a concrete priority to work towards. It does not mean managing every step of the job.

In the video below (15:28), Nick discusses how that change in approach fits the wider responsibility of leading a warehouse team.

## Agree what finished means, and when it matters

“Get the bay ready” leaves room for different interpretations. A clearer instruction identifies the area, the required result, and the finish time: move the overflow pallets to their assigned storage locations so the marked bay is empty before the 15:00 delivery.

Explaining why the time matters helps the supervisor weigh it against other work. If the delivery changes, they know which assumption to check. If the stock checks are also urgent, they can raise the conflict while there is still time to resolve it.

The supervisor can then plan the sequence with the team. Nick's broader point is that a manager who used to work on the floor has to make room for supervisors to make decisions. Knowing how you would do a task can help you coach someone; it can also make it tempting to take the job back whenever they approach it differently.

## Give the supervisor a priority they can act on

Suppose one operator has two hours available for the bay and the stock checks. Preparing the bay takes 30 minutes. The checks must be finished by shift end, but their duration depends on how many locations need attention.

Try changing the order below. Then allow more time for the stock checks. A plan that meets both deadlines with an hour of checks may stop working when the checks take longer.

Sometimes a different sequence is enough. Sometimes the assigned work cannot fit, even though everyone understands the deadlines. That needs a decision about cover, workload, or the delivery arrangement. Repeating the deadline will not resolve it.

Ask the supervisor how they plan to meet the commitments and what could prevent them. Agree which changes they can make themselves and which conflicts they should bring back to you. That gives them room to run the shift and a clear reason to involve you when the plan needs changing.
